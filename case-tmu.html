Case study · Applied research

A multi-institution research study, built to be checkable

Data Analyst · Together Design Lab, Toronto Metropolitan University · Dec 2025 – Present

A wide survey instrument, several partner institutions, four rounds of analysis, and a reporting deadline. The interesting problem was not the statistics — it was making sure that every number reaching a reader could be traced back to source data, and that the people who own the work could keep running it after I stopped touching it.

01 · Shape of the work

Six stages, each one auditable

The pipeline was built so that any figure in the final report can be walked backwards to the untouched export. Select a stage to see what happens there and what it hands to the next one.

02 · Cleaning

Cleaning as a record, not an edit

Nothing is changed in place. Every issue found becomes a row in a ledger — what was found, which rule caught it, what was done about it. The cleaned dataset is that ledger replayed over the raw export, which means the cleaning itself can be reviewed, argued with, and re-run.

Issues by class

Missing stays missing

The one class that does not go to zero is genuinely absent data. Quietly imputing it would make the dataset look cleaner than it is, so it is carried forward as missing and handled explicitly wherever it matters.

Re-runnable from scratch

A new export drops in and the whole pipeline replays. That mattered across four rounds — each round was a re-run, not a fresh round of manual work with fresh opportunities to slip.

Findable decisions

When a reviewer asks why a value looks the way it does, the answer is a ledger lookup rather than an archaeology exercise through someone's spreadsheet history.

03 · Geospatial

Maps the team can maintain without me

Several findings had a geographic dimension, so they became interactive maps (Leaflet, Folium, Plotly and ArcGIS depending on the output). The binding constraint was not cartography — it was that non-technical staff had to be able to refresh the data and republish after the engagement ended. Inputs are plain spreadsheets; the rebuild is one command.

04 · Verification

The part I would build again first

Reports go through many hands, and numbers drift — a figure gets updated in the text but not the appendix, or a chart is regenerated from a stale extract. So the harness reads the finished report, finds every number in it, and recomputes each one independently from source data. Anything that does not reconcile fails the build.

    05 · Access tiers

    Two audiences, one code path

    Partner institutions had different data-sharing constraints, so the same analysis had to produce outputs at two access levels. The trap here is maintaining two documents by hand until they disagree. Instead both tiers are generated from one pipeline with a different suppression threshold, so they cannot drift.

    06 · Tools

    What it was built with

    Python Pandas NumPy Leaflet Folium Plotly ArcGIS SQL Automated testing Stakeholder reporting

    Coordination was half the job

    Four rounds, academic supervisors, and partner institutions with different needs. The analytical plan had to survive contact with feedback, which meant designing for revision from the start rather than treating each round as a surprise.

    Handover was a requirement

    Tools that only I can run are a liability to the team that inherits them. Spreadsheet inputs and one-command rebuilds were a deliberate constraint, accepted at some cost to elegance.

    Trust is built, not asserted

    "I checked it" does not scale across four rounds and multiple reviewers. Automating the check is what let the team move quickly without quietly accumulating risk in the numbers.
